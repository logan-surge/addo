How animals score

Every animal has a rarity from 1 to 6 (how likely you are to see it at Addo) and a hype score from 1 to 5 (how loud the car gets). Points are 5 × rarity² × hype, so rare animals climb fast.

Lion: rarity 4, hype 5. 5 × 4² × 5 = 400 points.

Each animal scores once. If you see it again doing something better, tap it and update the sighting.

The list covers the mammals, birds and reptiles on SANParks’ Addo checklists, minus the marine species. Small things that are hard to tell apart, like bats, geckos and mice, share one entry each. Rarity scores are best guesses for a visit to the main game area, and some may live mainly in other sections of the park.

Multipliers

Tick these when you log a sighting. They stack.

  • I called it first: ×1.5
  • With a baby: ×2
  • On a night drive: ×2
  • Hunt, kill or fight: ×3

A lion on a kill, on a night drive, that you called first: 400 × 1.5 × 2 × 3 = 3,600.

Sets

    Bingo

    You get a Classic card and a Challenge card, and every phone deals its own. Classic mixes everyday sightings with a few trickier ones. Challenge mixes trickier ones with proper hard ones. Animal squares tick themselves when you log the animal. Tap any other square when it happens.

    • One line (row, column or diagonal): 100
    • Two lines: 250
    • Full Classic card: 1,000 bonus
    • Full Challenge card: 2,500 bonus

    House rules

    Everyone in the car can log the same animal, but only whoever genuinely saw it first takes the ×1.5. A Stick-o-saurus costs 10 points, and the car votes on it. Disputes go to the driver.

    Stats and facts

    Many animals have key stats and a few facts when you open them. Population numbers come from SANParks, the IUCN, BirdLife and other conservation groups. They’re estimates, and some are a few years old. “Status” is the animal’s worldwide IUCN Red List status.

    Photos

    Photos come from Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons, where photographers share them under free licences. Open any animal and tap “Photo credit and licence” under its photo to see who took it. A few entries, like Any bat or Any gecko, show one example species.

    The game downloads photos in the background when you have a decent connection and skips them on a slow one, so opening it never waits on them. All of them together are roughly 5 to 8 MB. You can turn downloads off, or start them yourself, in Settings on the Score tab.

    Leaderboard

    Your score uploads to the group leaderboard on the Score tab whenever you have signal, and you get everyone else’s back. With no signal it shows the last scores it saw, and catches up when you’re back in range. Scores are kept in the trip’s Google Sheet. Each phone counts as one player, and resetting the game keeps your spot.

    Using it with no signal

    Checking offline setup…

    iPhone: open the link in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.
    Android: open it in Chrome, tap the ⋮ menu, then Add to home screen or Install app.

    Do this while you still have signal, then always play from the home screen icon. On iPhone the icon keeps its own score, separate from Safari.

    Your score lives only on this phone, so clearing browser data or deleting the icon wipes it.
